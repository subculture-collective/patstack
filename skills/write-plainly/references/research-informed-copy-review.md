# Review copy for specificity and context

Research reviewed October 2, 2026; practitioner word list reviewed November 13, 2026. Apply this to marketing and interface copy or an explicitly requested deeper prose review. The checks below are editorial heuristics, not a classifier or a measure of conversion performance.

## Review and revise

Read the supplied copy alongside available product facts, audience language, brand voice, supported capabilities, and the intended action. Use reviews, interviews, support material, and product behavior when supplied; do not invent customer experience or retrieve private material outside the task's scope.

1. **Ground the claims.** Identify capability, outcome, quantitative, customer, and comparative claims. Keep supported claims and meaningful limits. Flag missing evidence or remove an unsupported assertion when that preserves the intended meaning. Never replace a vague claim with an invented statistic, testimonial, anecdote, urgency, or guarantee.
2. **Test specificity.** Substitute a competitor's name. Where the copy remains interchangeable, describe the actual task, mechanism, conditions, or consequence using known facts. A feature is useful when it explains the benefit; do not strip out the mechanism to make the copy sound promotional.
3. **Inspect repeated structures.** Look across the whole draft for repeated contrast formulas, self-answered questions ("The result? ..."), runs of short fragments, three-part lists, participial endings, abstract nouns, significance claims, and introductions/conclusions that repeat the same promise. Revise the pattern when it obscures meaning or adds no information. A single instance may be appropriate.
4. **Match the situation.** Keep the established voice while adjusting tone to the user's state. Errors need a supported explanation and next step; cancellation needs clarity about consequences; empty states need an achievable action. Avoid cheerful reassurance that conceals failure or unsupported promises about data safety.
5. **Check the action contract.** Button labels, eligibility, cost, trial conditions, and the next step must agree with known behavior. If runtime behavior is unavailable, state that limit rather than claim the journey was verified.
6. **Read the revision separately.** Check that each sentence contributes information, terminology stays consistent, and qualifications have not disappeared. Return the requested rewrite or review; do not expand a copy task into product changes or publication.

Illustrative example, assuming the product supports this behavior:

- Generic: “Streamline your workflow with our powerful, intuitive platform.”
- Specific: “Assign incoming support emails to a teammate and see who has replied.”

Treat word lists as prompts to inspect meaning. Terms such as “robust” may have a technical purpose. Long published lists ban ordinary words in every form, including words with exact technical senses: a `critical` alert, a test harness, navigating a UI, capturing packets, a stalled pipeline, a `void` return type. Ask whether the word names something specific in this sentence. If it does, keep it. If it does not, replacing it with a synonym fixes nothing; rewrite the sentence to state the fact. Em dashes, Oxford commas, contractions, and polished grammar are legitimate choices. Do not insert typos, forced slang, or fictional personal experience to imitate human authorship.

## Evidence and its limits

- [Reinhart et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16107v2): comparisons of GPT-4o and Llama 3 variants found differences in grammar and genre adaptation, including noun-heavy constructions and participial clauses. Instruction-tuned variants departed more from human patterns than base variants. These results do not describe every model or prompt.
- [Pew Research Center, August 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/data-labs/2026/08/20/how-much-of-the-internet-is-written-with-ai/): large-scale English web analysis found increased frequency of several AI-associated rhetorical and punctuation patterns. Population trends do not prove individual authorship; detector estimates are not ground-truth counts.
- [El Attar et al., June 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04177v1): lexical richness was the most consistently informative feature group across the tested models and domains. The benchmark excludes the newest models and systematic prompt variants. Do not reduce the result to “AI always has less vocabulary.”
- [Jakesch et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07271v4): human judgments of generated self-presentations relied on unreliable cues, including contractions and first-person language. Casual language is not proof of human authorship.
- [Liang et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02819v3): the tested detectors misclassified non-native English writing and could be bypassed through prompting. Do not treat predictable language as an accusation.
- [Geng and Trotta, 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.657/): changing word frequencies in academic abstracts were consistent with people adapting their model use and editing. Static blacklists are fragile.
- [ContentBeta, "List of 300+ AI Words, Phrases and Sentences to Avoid", last updated November 12, 2026, read November 13, 2026](https://www.contentbeta.com/blog/list-of-words-overused-by-ai/): a B2B marketing agency's practitioner list of about 150 words, ten contrast and reveal sentence templates, five corporate phrases and several habits (overdone metaphors, exaggeration, rhetorical questions). It cites no study or frequency data for the list, and it bans words such as "critical", "capture", "navigate" and "void" in every inflection. Rules 9 and 34 to 39 in the [pattern examples](upstream.md) take its sentence templates and its grouping of hype idioms, reveal lead-ins, stock openers, reception verbs and corporate abstractions. The word ban itself was not adopted.
- [Lin and Ma, 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.259/): a review-grounded copywriting method improved attractiveness and faithfulness against its tested baselines. Use authentic source material; this is not a universal claim about customer reviews or conversion rates.

These procedures are original editorial synthesis of the cited findings. No upstream skill body is imported.
